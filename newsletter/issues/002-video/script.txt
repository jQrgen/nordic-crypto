[s1 @ 29.8s, story]
The Red Party has proposed in the Storting a temporary halt for new data centres, with a licensing regime. It argues that crypto-mining data centres get power ahead of local industry. None of the proposals singles out crypto. That's from Stortinget.

[s2 @ 45.1s, story]
Socialist Left Party members have also proposed a pause on new data centres and a national licensing system, and ask the government to report on how the Storting's call for a crypto-mining ban will be followed up. This is a proposal, not a decision.

[s3 @ 58.4s, story]
In Ságat, the data-centre company Arctic Core says it uses crypto mining to earn income during a test phase, but argues this does not change the character of the building it has a permit for.

[s4 @ 68.5s, story]
Finansavisen reports that customers of the Norwegian exchange Firi bought 16.1 million kroner more crypto than they sold in September. Bitcoin was the top buy.

[s5 @ 78.7s, story]
Kaupr reports that Ace Digital, listed on Euronext Growth Oslo, made an unaudited third-quarter net profit of 6.3 million kroner, and says it now has no debt.

[b_se @ 88.5s, section - SWEDEN & EU]
(music/stinger only)

[s6 @ 90.5s, story]
Nyheter24 reports that Finansinspektionen has warned against thirty more firms operating without a licence, some offering trading in crypto. That brings this year's warnings to 227.

[s7 @ 102.2s, story]
And Finansinspektionen reports that the EU's anti-money-laundering authority, AMLA, has sent three draft technical standards to the European Commission. They include rules for crypto-asset service providers, and still need the Commission's adoption.
